>From a call to action to a call to acceleration

Since the first Action Agenda on Battery Systems in 2022, the Dutch battery ecosystem has clearly matured. New companies have joined, public-private investments have grown, and pilot and demonstration facilities have been developed. Dutch organisations have strengthened their position in European and global value chains.

The strategic importance of batteries has broadened at the same time. Beyond their role in the climate and energy transition, batteries are now essential for industrial competitiveness, economic resilience, energy security and strategic autonomy. The task is no longer to acquire a position in the battery market — it is to translate existing strengths faster into industrial and economic impact.

The Netherlands deliberately does not take part in every link of the global value chain. It focuses on areas with a distinctive 'right to play': advanced materials, production technology, system integration, circularity and application development, connected to a strong industrial base in heavy transport, energy systems and high-tech manufacturing.

"Not specific scale, but the coherence between these capabilities forms the basis for internationally competitive positions."
>

Seven coherent lines of action

1.Accelerate market access through validation, demonstration, launching customers and regulation.
2.Build the route from innovation to industrial production, scaling materials, production technology and equipment together.
3.Develop the digital system layer: battery management, data infrastructure, interoperability, validation and certification.
4.Develop a high-quality circular chain: life extension, repair, remanufacturing, repurposing and material recovery.
5.Strengthen shared infrastructure and financing: national test, pilot and production facilities plus Business Intelligence.
6.Strengthen talent and execution capacity, anchoring battery competences structurally in education.
7.Anchor the Netherlands in European value chains through international partnerships and EU programmes.
>01
Heavy Duty & Dual-Use
>

Electrifying heavy transport and dual-use applications

More than 30% of the trucks and buses for the European market are produced in the Netherlands. The country also has a substantial manufacturing and knowledge base in inland shipping, maritime, logistics and related industries — a clear right to play in battery systems for heavy land, sea and air vehicles. These markets are smaller in volume than passenger cars, yet strategically more important: they connect directly to Dutch logistics, ports, construction, defence and export industry. Value here is set less by price per kWh than by reliability, safety, lifetime, energy density, certification, maintainability and supply security. For aviation, drones and dual-use applications, safe European supply chains and high-quality system integration are decisive. Because these markets demand so much, the Netherlands distinguishes itself with knowledge-intensive systems and flexible production rather than generic mass production.

Where NL excels
  • Leading OEMs: DAF, Damen, VDL, Ebusco, Scania, HysterYale
  • Specialised suppliers and system integrators
  • Maritime, inland-navigation and aviation expertise
  • Charging infrastructure for heavy transport
Where NL focuses
  • Battery systems for heavy-duty vehicles and vessels
  • Dual-use design: shared civil and military specifications
  • Batteries as digitally managed assets (BMS, digital twins)
  • Value retention: serviceability, revision, second life

>02
Stationary Applications
>

Storing renewable energy, stabilising the grid

The market for stationary storage is growing fast. In the Netherlands that growth is driven by electrification and renewable generation from sun and wind — and by grid congestion, which increasingly blocks new production, storage and consumption sites. Grid reinforcement and demand response are necessary but not sufficient: electricity storage has become an essential part of the future energy system. Stationary battery systems span utility-scale installations at solar and wind parks, industrial and commercial storage, energy hubs, neighbourhood and home batteries, and mobile systems. A distinction is made between short-duration storage (SDES, 1–4 hours) and long-duration storage (LDES, from roughly 8 hours to multiple days). Data, software, cybersecurity, safety engineering, testing and certification form an integral, distinguishing part of the Dutch proposition.

Where NL excels
  • Grid congestion solutions and energy management
  • System integration expertise
  • Storage innovation: Aquabattery, Battolyser, Elestor, Exergy, Alfen
  • Renewable energy infrastructure and VPP platforms
Where NL focuses
  • Short- and long-duration energy storage (SDES / LDES)
  • Launch market for LDES on non-critical, abundant materials
  • Integration with renewable generation and energy hubs
  • Bi-directional charging and grid services

>03
(Next Generation) Components & Materials
>

Pioneering the materials that power tomorrow's batteries

Innovations in battery components and materials determine the next generation: higher energy density, longer lifetime and better safety, but also lower cost, more sustainable production and less dependence on critical raw materials. Several technological routes develop in parallel; some materials are close to commercial application while others are still in research or pilot phase. Because no single battery technology serves every application optimally, space opens for diversification — matched to energy density, lifetime, charging speed, safety and material availability. Active materials account for a substantial share of both the cost and the value creation of batteries, so control of core technology and intellectual property is strategic.

Where NL excels
  • Silicon-anode technology (LeydenJar)
  • Solid-state batteries (LionVolt)
  • Thin-film & atomic-layer deposition (SALD, SparkNano)
  • Bulk-type batteries (Aquabattery, Battolyser)
  • Materials research: TU Delft, TU/e, University of Groningen, UTwente
Where NL focuses
  • Alternative and sustainable battery materials
  • Scaling production equipment for cell components
  • R&D and pilot facilities for pre-industrialisation
  • Niche-to-market: consumer electronics and drones toward defence, heavy duty, aviation, automotive

>04
Production Equipment
>

Building the machines that build batteries

Production equipment is needed across the entire battery value chain: from materials, cells and packs to maintenance, reuse and recycling. The world market for battery production equipment is expected to grow from about USD 15.6 billion in 2025 to nearly USD 37 billion by 2030. The Dutch high-tech manufacturing industry has strong competences in precision technology, mechatronics, digitalisation and advanced production systems — yet is still only limitedly connected to the battery market. Asian parties dominate large-scale, efficient equipment for conventional cells. The Netherlands cannot easily catch up on price and volume. The more promising strategy: flexible, reconfigurable, software-defined production lines that serve current and next-generation technologies, where performance, quality and innovation power lead instead of output alone.

Where NL excels
  • High-tech manufacturing and mechatronics
  • Precision technology and systems engineering
  • Digitalisation and AI-driven process control
  • Automation and scaling of assembly lines
Where NL focuses
  • Modular, reconfigurable, software-defined production systems
  • Retrofit of existing European lines for Li-ion, solid-state, silicon anodes
  • Ultra-efficient, AI-driven cell production
  • European and global validation and sales partnerships

>05
Circular Value Chain
>

Life extension, repurposing and high-quality recycling

The growth of batteries in mobility, energy systems, industry and consumer products drives strong demand for lithium, nickel, cobalt, copper and graphite — while the number of batteries reaching end-of-life rises sharply. The Netherlands, a European leader in circular material use, combines a favourable geographic position with expertise in diagnostics, life extension and recycling. The chain runs from inspection, repair and remanufacturing to second-life systems and material recovery from black mass. Dutch processes use less energy and fewer chemicals and hold strong positions in diagnostics — integrated with European refining, material and battery production chains. Enablers: reliable battery data and the digital battery passport, standardised diagnostics and certification, and sufficient recyclable feedstock.

Where NL excels
  • Diagnostics, testing and life-extension expertise
  • High-quality recycling organisations (ARN, Back to Battery)
  • European logistics position for end-of-life streams
  • Digital battery passport development (Stichting OPEN)
Where NL focuses
  • Repair, remanufacturing and second-life business models
  • Efficient recovery of Li, Ni, Co, Cu and graphite from black mass
  • Automated safe pre-processing: discharging, disassembly, assessment
  • Harmonised test, diagnosis and certification methods

>Strategic overarching themes

Besides the five focus themes, the Actieagenda identifies seven ecosystem-wide themes. Together they form the foundation on which the Dutch battery ecosystem can grow: human capital, demonstration and scale-up capacity, data, safety and testing, the battery passport, European cooperation and business intelligence — accelerating innovation, industrialisation and market application across the board.

>FacilitiesDemonstration, pilot, scale-up and production capacity shared across the ecosystem.
>Data, Safety & ValidationTesting, certification and safe development as a shared gateway to market.
>Digital Battery PassportThe data-driven backbone of a transparent, circular battery chain.
>Battery Cell ManufacturingA route toward competitive Dutch and European cell production.
>Human CapitalStructural anchoring of battery competences in education and the labour market.
>Business IntelligenceMonitoring, market intelligence and evidence for policy and investment.
>European & International CooperationStrategic partnerships and active participation in EU programmes.
>One compassGovernment, industry and knowledge institutions, moving in one direction.
